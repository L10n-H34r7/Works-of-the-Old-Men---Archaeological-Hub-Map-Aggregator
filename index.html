🏛️ Works of the Old Men

Archaeological Hub & Map Aggregator for Ancient Arabian Stone Structures

About This Project

What are the "Works of the Old Men"?

The "Works of the Old Men" (Arabic: أعمال الأولين) is the name given by Bedouin tribes to the vast arrays of ancient stone structures found across the Arabian Peninsula—from Syria and Jordan in the north, through Saudi Arabia, to Yemen and Oman in the south, extending across the Red Sea into Djibouti and Ethiopia.

First brought to scholarly attention by RAF pilots in the 1920s, these structures number in the hundreds of thousands and include:

  • Desert Kites — Massive hunting traps with long driving walls converging on an enclosure
  • Wheels — Circular stone arrangements with spokes, likely funerary/ritual
  • Pendants — Strings of burial cairns with tail structures
  • Mustatils / Gates — Monumental rectangular structures (20–600m long)
  • Meandering Walls — Long, sinuous stone walls
  • Cairns & Tombs — Burial structures of various forms

Data Sources

  • GlobalKites Project (CNRS/Université Lyon 2) — 6,721+ desert kites [Zenodo]
  • EAMENA Community (Oxford/Leicester/Durham) — Endangered archaeology across MENA [Zenodo]
  • Wasfy Mustatils Complex — Mustatil & pendant structures, Central Arabia [Zenodo]
  • Western Nafud Survey — NW Saudi Arabia & SE Jordan [Zenodo]

Purpose

This hub aggregates dispersed datasets onto one unified map for researchers, heritage professionals, and enthusiasts. Use the Layers panel to toggle datasets and the Tools panel to import new data, mark structures, or export.
